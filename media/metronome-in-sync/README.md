# Wizard — Every source, one answer

Current cut: **121 seconds / 2:01, 1920×1080, 30 fps**, English narration and background music, no subtitles, light mode. Revision 14 is a concept film for review. It tells one story for a non-technical audience: our data is spread across many portals and files, and Wizard brings it together so anyone can ask a question in plain words and get a checked, visual answer. From the question on, the screens are **the real Wizard app**, recorded and shown in real time; only the stretch where Wizard is working is sped up, as a marked timelapse. The narration carries the app scenes: it says what we are going to ask while the question is typed, and what Wizard is doing while it works. The camera moves slowly and seldom, and Wizard's stage holds still while it works. Fast moves carry motion blur, the key statements type in letter by letter, and there are no sound effects.

The narration is voiced in **en-US-AndrewMultilingualNeural**, a newer and more natural model of the Andrew voice used up to revision 13 (the owner found the old one robotic and chose this one from samples).

## Story

1. **Data everywhere:** ASAP, GSCM, BDP and NERP, each packed with reports, plus Excel files, emails, presentations and PDFs. No counts are given; the lists rush past instead.
2. **Hunting for one answer:** the question types in large over the blurred clutter, docks into its search bar, and someone opens report after report while the clock runs.
3. **Everything in Wizard:** every source flows into Wizard's logo; its local AI knows where each answer lives.
4. **Just ask (real app):** Wizard's start screen grows out of the logo. "In this example, we're going to ask: how did we do this quarter against our targets?" — the question is typed as it is said, then "Q3 targets.xlsx" is attached and the question sent, in real time.
5. **Wizard at work:** "First, it makes a plan." The pixel wizard appears and writes its plan in real time; about three seconds later the timelapse starts (a "Timelapse 2×" pill with the run's real clock, and a blue frame edge), and GSCM, ASAP, NERP and the Excel file light up as the narrator names them, then "it runs the numbers, draws a chart, and writes up the answer". The stage holds still throughout.
6. **The answer:** a soft dissolve to the answer's top; the camera eases from the summary down to the chart.
7. **A second question**, cross-fading in: "Next, let's ask: where is stock building up in the channel?", typed in real time; while its run plays at 4× the narrator explains that Wizard compares what we shipped in with what sold, model by model, from GSCM; the answer: Egypt's Galaxy A37, whose stock has more than doubled.
8. **Checked**, in the same window: "To be sure, we scroll down and click Check my data", in real time; the check runs as a 2× timelapse while Wizard goes back to GSCM and works every figure out again; its six MATCH rows hold under "Every number is checked against its source report", and the Sources drawer opens on the figure's own rows.
9. **Safe:** the app steps back behind "Secure by design", its three lines on the owner's security line.
10. **Stop searching. Start asking.** The statement pops in word by word, whips off as Wizard's pixel logo punches in, and "Every source. One answer." types in above every source.

## Build

The film is HTML rendered by pinned HyperFrames 0.8.142 (Node 24). Edit `compositions/wizard.js`, `compositions/film.css`, `compositions/runtime.js`, `scripts/screen.py` and `narration.json`.

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python audio\voice.py rev14            # voice every line in bank rev14 (needs speech.platform.bing.com)
.venv\Scripts\python audio\remix.py                  # narration from the voice banks and the music bed; writes assets/cues.json and audio/score.wav
.venv\Scripts\python scripts\screen.py --report      # when each recorded moment appears; place narration.json lines from it
.venv\Scripts\python scripts\screen.py               # cut capture/takes into assets/screen/*.mp4 and assets/screen.json
python scripts/subtitles.py                           # Korean subtitles from narration.json: assets/subtitles-ko.json, subtitles/wizard-ko.vtt and .srt
node scripts/build.mjs                                # timeline.json, index.html and index-ko.html with the cues, motion times and camera
.\film.ps1 check --at '3,7.6,8,14,15,15.6,16.5,21.5,22.2,23.3,26,30,34.3,35,38,40,43,45,47,49,51,53,56,58.5,60,60.6,62.5,65,67.2,68.3,69,71,73,75,77.5,80,82.5,83.3,85,88,90.7,92.5,94,96,97,99,101,104,105.6,107,109,111,113,114.2,115.2,116.4,117.2,118.4,120' --json
.\film.ps1 render --fps 30 --quality looks --workers 4 --output renders/review/animatic.mp4
.\film.ps1 render --composition index-ko.html --fps 30 --quality looks --workers 4 --output renders/review/animatic-ko.mp4
node scripts/serve-review.mjs
```

- **Voice.** Every line is cut from a line bank in the film's voice (en-US-AndrewMultilingualNeural at −3%, the owner's pronunciation: ASAP as a word, NERP as "N-ERP", smooth GSCM, BDP and PDF). `audio/voice.py rev14` voices every line whose `voice` is `rev14` (edge-tts 7.3.1; 7.2.8's client token is now refused) into `audio/voice-rev14.flac`, with each word's offset in `audio/voice-rev14.json`. `audio/remix.py` cuts each line from its bank and places it at its start in `narration.json`, so no speech service is needed once a line is voiced; `audio/score.py` re-voices every line instead. A new or reworded line can go into a new bank so the others keep their recordings. `remix.py --draft` builds before a bank exists: those lines are silent, and their length and word cues are estimated (about 0.22 s a syllable), so the picture can be timed first. Lines are placed by their key words: each question's first word as its first letter is typed, "sell-out", "share", "spend" and "targets" as Wizard reads GSCM, ASAP, NERP and the file, "click" just before the click; re-run `screen.py --report` after moving one.
- **Screens.** `scripts/screen.py` describes each clip as a path through a recorded take: steps at real speed, a timelapse step only while Wizard works (from about three seconds after it appears until its stage gives way to the answer), stretches reached by a cue, and dissolving cuts. Capture moments are recorder markers, tool calls as Wizard logged them, or the moment a run's stage scrolls away for its answer (from the recorder's stage log); `--report` prints when each appears in the film, which is how the narration lines are placed. There are two takes and two windows: question 1, then question 2 with its check, so the check runs in the window that shows the answer it checks. Frames are the capture's own 1600 × 900 screencast frames, scaled to 3200 × 1800 once. The clips need `capture/takes/` frames (not committed: record them with [capture](capture/README.md)); `--camera-only` rewrites the camera without re-encoding.
- **Stage lock.** Wizard keeps the newest line of a conversation in view, so whenever a tool starts or ends and the stage's status line comes or goes, the page scrolls about 23 px; sped up, that read as jitter. While Wizard works, `screen.py` follows the stage's top edge frame by frame (seeded from the recorder's stage log, then matched picture to picture, and checked against the log) and moves each frame by that much, so the stage holds still. The frames are only moved, never redrawn, and the camera is checked never to show the fixed title bar or composer that the move shifts. When Check my data is clicked, the answer twitches twice and Wizard's new card lands with a two-frame bounce; the path cuts 0.2 s after the click straight to the settled card (0.3 s of capture skipped).
- **Glides.** Twice Wizard's page scrolls itself in real time (down to Check my data, back up to the figure). The screencast delivers about 40 frames a second, so sampled at 30 the scroll moved in uneven one- and two-frame steps. `screen.py` measures each capture frame's scroll by matching it to the one before and places every film frame's conversation area at the scroll the page had reached at that instant, from the two frames either side; the pointer, which rests during the scroll, stays put.
- **Camera.** A few keyframes per clip, each a page point at the frame centre and a zoom. `screen.py` turns them into window placements and interpolates those with a monotone cubic, so the camera eases into and out of holds, passes through the others without stopping and never overshoots; it writes the placement for every frame into `assets/screen.json`, the zoom to five decimals (rounded to two, a slow push-in stepped 1% about every second, which read as jitter). It fails the build if a close framing would show the window's edge (which the runtime used to clamp, jerking the camera) or if a stage lock would bring a fixed bar into frame. The page moves on its own when Wizard scrolls, so the camera holds while it does.
- **Motion.** `scripts/build.mjs` works out when every move starts (`motion` in `timeline.json`); `compositions/runtime.js` animates from those times: kinetic type pops each letter in larger, nudged and soft, then settles; motion blur is a directional Gaussian per moving element, sized every frame from how far it moved, so slow moves stay sharp. Question 2 cross-fades in where question 1 was. There are no sound effects.
- **Music.** `audio/bed.py` trims or extends the track for both mixers. The film is longer than the 87.6 s track, so at 0:45, 1:02 and 1:19, under narration, it crossfades back eight bars (16.865 s at about 114 bpm) to the matching beat.
- **Korean subtitles.** Each line in `narration.json` carries its Korean subtitle in `ko`; a `|` splits a long line into parts, and `koSplit` names the English word whose spoken start brings in the next part, so the subtitle changes as the narrator moves on. `scripts/subtitles.py` times them from the voice bank (each line until it ends, plus a short hold, never into the next) and writes `assets/subtitles-ko.json` and, for players, `subtitles/wizard-ko.vtt` and `.srt`. `build.mjs` writes `index-ko.html`, the same film with the subtitles drawn in Pretendard on a dark backing, 24 px above the bottom (clear of the opening's file cards); whenever the camera shows Wizard's composer they rise above it, and a line marked `koTop` (the click on Check my data, at the bottom of the frame) is shown at the top. `index.html` is unchanged by them.
- **Rendering.** HyperFrames decodes the clips with FFmpeg, frame-accurately. On Linux, point `HYPERFRAMES_BROWSER_PATH` at a chrome-headless-shell; on Windows, its bundled headless browser (`hyperframes browser ensure`) avoids opening desktop Chrome. Previewing in a browser needs one that plays H.264 (Chrome, Edge).

## Scope and fidelity

A concept film. Sections 1–3 and the end card are drawn for the film; sections 4–9 are the real Wizard web app (`main` at `ff4a9e2`), running locally in replay mode over Wizard's synthetic fixtures with Wizard's own tools, recorded and sped up. For the recording, Wizard's development labels for replay and synthetic data and its demo suggestions are hidden, and the film carries "Real Wizard app · illustrative data" instead. Figures come from synthetic data; the targets file is synthetic. The narration says "local AI" and "authorized AI" at the owner's direction. No accuracy or time-saving claim is measured. Typing, clicks, scrolling and the answers landing play in real time; the timelapse clock shows how long the recorded run took, paced by the capture server, not by a live Gemini.
